# Objects: real heights (`scenes/objects/art/piece_heights.gd`)

Side file of `domain-objects`; interfaces 11, 12 and 28. The meshes (`meshes.md`) are built to these.

## Real heights

The meshes' sizes are real: 1 px a 3D unit, 42.3 px a metre (`BodyHeights`, `docs/research/3d-cover-heights.md`), so every `ObjectMesh*Config` height is a real height and no piece is drawn out of proportion with a 74 px person: a kitchen counter 38 (0.90 m) with a 6 px backsplash, a dresser, cabinet and tool chest 38, a nightstand 26, a washer and dryer 38, a chair back 38, a plant 40, a barricade 40, a crate 20 (a stack 40), a filing cabinet 56 (its `art_opts.height`), a table 31, a coffee table 18, a bench 19, a workbench 38 (their `art_opts.height`), a bookshelf 77, a fridge 74, a stove 38, a floor lamp 62, a coat rack 72, a shower's glass 80, a bar counter 40 with its stool 27 and a booth's back 46, a drinks fridge 80, a snack machine 76, a vending machine 78, a locker 77, a wrapped pallet 45, a street bench 19, a bin 40, a car prop 60 (roof; the drivable car is `VehicleMeshConfig`'s 36 / 52, aligned with the vehicles). An awning's valance (80) and a hanging sign's blade (78 to 98) clear a standing head (74); the wall is 96 and a door leaf 84 as before.

`PieceHeights` (interface 28: its cost and who reads it) is the read of them: `top_of(definition)` / `base_of(definition)` / `parts_of(definition)` for a catalogue row, `top_of_spec` / `parts(spec)` for a `FurnitureSpec`. It asks each mesh script's own `height_of(spec)` (table, cabinet, shelf, appliance, counter, noodle, clinic, security, shop, stock, street prop) or the holder's value, so the mesh and its height are one number; a multi-part piece lists its parts (`{ base, top, rect }`, `rect` the share of the footprint in the canonical frame): a sofa's seat, two arms and back; a chair's seat and back; a bed's mattress and headboard; a counter's top and backsplash; a noodle booth's seats, backs and table; a hung piece (mirror, towel rack, menu board) starts at its base. The top is the piece's mass: a prop on it (a bowl on a bar, a coat rack's hat, a till, a surgical chair's lamp arm) is left out. There is no catalogue `coverage` column: a shot, a burst and the AI's cover judgement all read these heights. Test: `tests/objects/piece_heights_test.gd` (every catalogue kind has a top inside the real metres of its kind; its built mesh agrees: a face at each part's top inside its rect, props allowed).

**Cover and a duck.** What is built as cover hides a ducked head (36 px, `BodyHeights.DUCK`) by at least 0.04 m (the barricade was 36 and hid it by 0.06 px; now 40), and no solid piece on the floor tops out within 0.04 m of that head either way (a chair back, a plant, a washer, a crate stack were all at 36 to 37 and moved). `piece_heights_test.gd` pins both.

**The wall fade.** `WallFadeConfig.tall_height` is 48: a piece under it is never looked at. The retune moved two kinds across it: a dresser, cabinet and tool chest (56 to 38) no longer fade, the shower's glass (38 to 80) now does; a wardrobe, a fridge and a bookshelf still do. `tests/art/wall_fade_furniture_test.gd` pins a dresser (never faded), a wardrobe and a shower.

## The twins (`scenes/objects/object_twins.gd`, interface 29)

`ObjectTwins.register(space)` (called by `MatchBuilder._twin_space`) adds the hook that builds Objects' twins in General's `TwinSpace` and has it twin the `floors` group. A wall is a `StaticBody3D` over its 2D colliders lifted to `ObjectStructureMeshConfig.wall_height` (96); a door a `StaticBody3D` over its leaf to `door_height` (84), written with the leaf's transform whenever the leaf turns (a script turns it, so the twin is a frame behind); a floor a `floor_thickness` slab whose top is `floor_lift` (a road `-ObjectStreetMeshConfig.kerb`, `StreetGround.is_road`), placed from the floor's corner; a solid piece a body with one box per `PieceHeights.parts` (each part's `rect` in the canonical frame, turned by `FurnitureSpec.turns` to the piece's facing; a round piece's whole-footprint part is a cylinder): a `StaticBody3D` too, written with the piece's transform when a shove or a turn moves it (a frozen piece is never followed, to revisit when furniture goes 3D). A non-solid piece (a rug) has no twin. All sit on `PhysicsLayers.TWIN_WORLD`. A dent that rebuilds a 2D collider does not reshape the twin. Tests: `tests/objects/twin_walls_floors_test.gd`, `twin_sofa_parts_test.gd` (parts at their heights, turned with the facing), `twin_furniture_follows_test.gd`, `twin_door_swing_test.gd`.
