# The textured toon (`post/toon_tex.gdshader`, `ToonMaterial.textured`, `MeshNodes.textured`)

The 2D painters' art, painted unshaded, on a mesh, shaded by the toon bands. One planar projection straight down in MODEL space, `uv_rect` (an instance uniform, set in units from the px `rect`) the footprint the art covers; a top face (normal up) shows the art, a side face the art's edge texels stretched down and darkened a step (`MeshKitConfig.textured_side_dim`), a clear texel the vertex colour; `opts.tiled` repeats the art (floor, road); `opts.cutout` draws nothing where the art is clear (a flat sprite laid over solids, as a character's head art over its dome). One material per tiling and cut-out for every texture in the atlas (below), so nodes sharing a mesh draw in one call whatever their art; `ToonMaterial.textured_count()` counts every atlased texture still alive, shown or not (the paint caches keep them; a warmed one is shown by no node yet), plus those with a material of their own. An opaque texel is the art times `base_color` only (the vertex colour is NOT multiplied in: a mesh with coloured parts, as a furniture mesh, keeps its tones for where the art is clear); tint with `base_color`.

The light, bands, fade and vision read are `toon_common.gdshaderinc`, shared with `toon.gdshader` and `decal.gdshader`; `toon_surface.gdshaderinc` holds `base_color`, `figure`, `emissive`. Tests: `tests/art/mesh_textured_suite.gd`, `toon_tex_windowed_test.gd` (`TEXTOON_SHOTS=<dir>` saves the shots), `toon_atlas_same_picture_windowed_test.gd` (a district drawn with the atlas and with a material per texture).

## The atlas (`post/toon_atlas.gd`, `ToonAtlas`)

Godot 4.4's instance uniforms cannot be samplers, so the painted textures are packed into the layers of one `Texture2DArray`, and each node says where its own lies (the instance uniform `atlas_rect`, set by `textured`). A layer is a page of `PixelPostConfig.atlas_page` (512) texels a side, filled in shelves. The shader reads it by `texelFetch`, clamped to the texture's own texels, so no neighbour bleeds in; against a material per texture only pixels that fall exactly between two texels may differ.

- **Copied once**, the first time a node shows the texture (`slot`: a `get_image` readback). **Never update a shown texture in place**: the atlas keeps the old texels. `MeshNodes.shown_texture(node)` says what a node shows.
- **Written once a frame**, before it is drawn (`flush`): the changed layers (1 MB each), or the whole array when it grows (`GROW`, 8 layers).
- **Warmed at match build**, so play copies nothing: `MeshNodes.warm_textured(texture)` for what is first shown mid-match (every item's held and lying art and the spoon, from `ItemArt.prepaint`; every burst's smoke, from `PhysicsMeshes.install`; a glass door's shattered leaf, from `DoorMesh.build`), and `ToonAtlas.reserve()` (Main's view, once the match is shown) leaves at least `GROW` spare layers, so a page opened in play is written alone. A new thing first shown mid-match warms its art the same way. There is no per-frame bound.
- The atlas holds no texture alive (the nodes do, `PixelArt`'s cache too); a freed one's place goes to the next texture of its size. The pages stay for the process, across matches.
- A texture larger than a page (`fits`) has a material and `albedo_texture` of its own, weakly cached as a decal's.
- A shader or caller using `ToonMaterial.textured` directly must also set `atlas_rect`: call `MeshNodes.textured`.

## Rules and limits

- **Top or side:** a face is a top when its model normal has `y > 0.5` (a slope within 60 degrees of flat), a side otherwise; an underside (never seen from above) takes the side rule. The kit's faces are flat, so it is decided per face (`flat` varyings). A tiled side wraps along the axis it runs along (by its normal's larger horizontal part), so a long road or wall side repeats the art's edge.
- **Model space:** the art follows the node's yaw, scale and stretch (model space is the rest pose). A `Skeleton3D`-skinned mesh would slide the art over its bones: none is used today.
- **Units:** `uv_rect` is baked from `RenderConfig.unit` when `textured` is called, not read live; call it again after a unit change. One `uv_rect` per node, so a MultiMesh's parts share it.
- **Cost:** never call `textured` per frame with a fresh texture: each new texture is a readback and a layer write. Hold the texture (a piece's flat render is cached by `PixelArt`) and call once when the mesh is shown.
- **Tint:** a node never tinted is white; a tint it already has stays unless `opts.tint` names one.

## The toon light (`post/toon_common.gdshaderinc`, `toon_light.gd`, `toon_light_config.gd`)

One `light()` for all three toon shaders: a pixel is its painted colour times the dusk ambient (the global `dusk_ambient`, `ToonLight.set_ambient`, set by Lighting; `ToonLightConfig.default_ambient` until it is) plus each light's amount (N.L, times its fall-off and shadow, times `seen`) stepped into bands and tinted by `LIGHT_COLOR`: the sun's into `PixelPostConfig.bands` bands of `ToonLightConfig.levels`, a positional light's pool through `pool_levels` (eight, from a trace, 0.08, to the light whole, times `pool_strength`) as a smooth curve, each level at its band's middle, so a pool fades with no ring, `toon_pool`. A pool is Godot's fall-off made linear again and as flat as the slope in the light's `light_specular` says (`SPECULAR_AMOUNT = ToonLight.specular(slope, kind, code, height, rim) = slope + kind / 4 + 4 * (code + 1024 * (steps + 64 * class))`, kind 0 a lamp, 1 a flash, 2 a white-out: the toon has no specular; `code` is the light's room and street flag, which `toon_reach` reads against the spot's room, `ToonLight.set_rooms`, so a pool stops at its room's walls; `steps` is the light's height over its pool's rim in sixteenths and `class` its height on a 1.4 ladder from 4: the pool is rebuilt from the ground distance, `r = pow(1 - sqrt(ATTENUATION), 0.25)` being the distance in 3D over the range, less the fragment's own height `spot.z`, so a light hung high pools as a low one does). The sum is held to `ToonLightConfig.cap` by its brightest channel, so the hue stays (a flash is added apart from it, in `SPECULAR_LIGHT`: `docs/lighting.md` L9-3D; the ambient is the dusk's colour in linear times `ambient_gain`, `ToonLight.shader_ambient`). The ambient is added in the one directional light's call (each view has one: the sky), the sun scaled by `spot.x`, the sky mask (`ToonLight.set_sky`: only where it is white; Godot allows no varying write in a helper and no `VERTEX` in `light()`, so each `fragment()` stores `spot = toon_spot(VERTEX, INV_VIEW_MATRIX)`, a `vec3`: the sun's reach, the room and whether the spot is above the floor, and its world height). Defaults: the sun everywhere, no room clipping. Test: `lighting/light_3d_look_windowed`.
