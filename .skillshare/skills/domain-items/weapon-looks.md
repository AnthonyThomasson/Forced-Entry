# Weapon looks and item art

An item is shown by its `art` part (`ItemArt`, `item_art.gd`; built once with the item, holding the gun or grenade id it draws and what else it needs, never the item). The base draws nothing (fists, unarmed, key, magazine); `GunArt`, `GrenadeArt` and `SpoonArt` give the rest. Painters live in `scenes/items/art/`, Items-private; the Art domain's engine is `domain-art`.

## Weapon style

- `ItemArt.style` is the **weapon style** the item is drawn in, its wielder's: `police` (both police units, the default), or `scrap` / `pipe` per defender (`CharacterStyleConfig.styles`' `weapons`). `ItemRegistry.inventory_for(..., style := &"police")` stamps it on every item it makes; `LoadoutConfig.apply` passes `style.weapon` through `Character.inventory.set_loadout`.
- **A style never changes what an item does.** `GunConfig`, `GrenadeConfig`, holds, muzzle points, raised-barrel lengths and sounds are the kind's, so every style sits where every other does. Tests: `tests/items/weapon_style_function_test.gd` (the guard), `weapon_style_look_test.gd`, `weapon_style_kept_test.gd`, `tests/general/match_styles_test.gd`.
- A gun or grenade draws its config row's `looks` row for the style: `GunConfig.look(gun, style)` / `GrenadeConfig.look(kind, style)` (`default_style` `police` for a style the row has none for). A look row is the `painter`, its colours, per-style marks and the `finish`. Every gun shares the three gun looks (`GunConfig._looks()`); each grenade kind has its own.
- One painter per style and kind: `police_*`, `scrap_*`, `pipe_*` as `*_gun_painter.gd` and `*_grenade_painter.gd`, sharing `weapon_art.gd` (`paint_gun`, `paint_grenade`, the two gun silhouettes, the marks' helpers), so a style painter keeps only its flags (`crude`, `wood_stock`, `wood_grip`) and marks. The popped spoon is the same in every style (`spoon_painter.gd`).

## Look handle

A painter's options carry the look as an int (`opts.look`, `PixelArt.handle`, read back with `PixelArt.look_of`), never the row, because `PixelArt` hashes the options on every call. An item registers its row once (`ItemArt._look_handle`, again only when the row drawn changes). The weapon-style tests compare what the art hands out (`held_mesh`, `held_texture`, `dropped_mesh`, `dropped_texture`: one instance per look).

## Gun art

The painters draw in the canonical frame (muzzle toward +y, butt at y = 0) and scale from `c.w` / `c.h`; the 3D render stands the flat render up (below). Hand 0 is the weapon hand. Firing calls `user.weapon.kick(weapon_hand(), recoil)`; the supporting hand follows by its stance's `recoil_share`. A gun raised off a wall is pitched by `GunArt.held_lift` (`RAISED_LENGTH`: the foreshortened barrel length at full raise).

## Prepainting

`ItemRegistry.inventory_for` asks each item it makes for `art.prepaint()`: it asks for the item's held and loose meshes and flat art (the base does it for every kind; an item that shows nothing gets nulls), so `MeshCache` and `PixelArt`'s flat cache hold them before a fight and no first build or paint (0.3 to 0.5 ms) lands in its busiest frames; the art goes into the toon's atlas too (`MeshNodes.warm_textured`; a grenade's prepaint takes the spoon's with it). Test: `tests/items/prepaint_covers_the_draws_test.gd` (a match's asks build and paint nothing new, and the art is atlased).

## The held item in 3D (`ItemArt.held_mesh`, `GunMesh`, `GrenadeMesh`, `ItemSolid`, `ItemMeshConfig`)

In the 3D render a character's body shows what it holds by the 2D art stood up: a mesh Items raises from the painter's own flat render and the same art projected straight down onto it. Character places it (`domain-character/body-mesh.md`); Items makes no node and touches no shader. `item.art.held_mesh() -> Mesh` (null: nothing, the default for fists, unarmed, key), `held_texture()` (the painter's `PixelArt.render_flat`, `domain-art/flat-paint.md`) and `held_rect()` (where the art lies in the mesh's frame, the `rect` of `MeshNodes.textured`, `domain-art/textured-toon.md`: `BodyHeld` calls it when the item changes, never per frame), `held_aim(user)` (the flat direction it points: the weapon's facing, a punch's turn) and `held_lift(user)` (radians pitched up: a gun raised off a wall, `acos` of the foreshortened length).

- **A gun** (`GunMesh.held`): the coverage of the flat render of the held painter (the pixels it drew, `ItemSolid.rows`) lifted to the height of the part covering each row (`WeaponArt.HELD_PARTS`, the heights its 2D shadows use, times `ItemMeshConfig.thickness`), a round barrel (`ItemSolid.bar`: a cylinder as wide as the art there, `BARREL_FACETS` faces) over the rows from the barrel part on. So the footprint IS the 2D art's (`art_size`, the butt `grip_back` behind the origin) and seen from above it is the 2D gun; a part's height shows only from the side and in its shadow.
- **A grenade** (`GrenadeMesh.of`): the flat render turned about its length (a radius per picture row, `ItemMeshConfig.grenade_sides` faces, so the egg, the cylinder and the narrower cap follow the art), the lever held down a strip over the pixels the render with `spoon` adds over the one without, standing `lever_height` off the shell.
- Frame: the origin is the weapon hand's grip, the muzzle (the fuze) leads along +z, up is +y; a gun's underside is `held_base` below the hand's height so it sits about the hand, a grenade's axis is at it, its middle `grenade_ahead` ahead as the 2D art draws it.
- One mesh per kind and look through `MeshCache` (the key holds the row's size, the painter, the look's colours and the tuning), one texture through `PixelArt`'s flat cache, each texture once in the toon's atlas (`ToonMaterial.textured_count()`). A new row needs no mesh code. Tests: `tests/items/item_mesh_from_art_suite.gd` (sizes, parts, one mesh per look), `tests/character/character_held_mesh_test.gd`; the pictures, `character/character_held_mesh_windowed` (`HELD_SHOTS`, `HELD_PITCH`).

## Loose items in 3D (`ItemMeshes`, `LyingItemMesh`, `ItemArt.dropped_mesh` / `dropped_texture` / `dropped_rect`; #193)

A `DroppedItem` (a dead hand's gun, a grenade's spoon: group `dropped_items`) and a `ThrownGrenade` (group `live_grenades`, in the air and lying) are shown by a `LyingItemMesh`, which Main's view plugs in with `ItemMeshes.register(follower)` (`MeshFollower.track_group`). It is the mesh the item's art gives for a loose item, `dropped_mesh(flipped)`, wearing `dropped_texture(flipped)` (`dropped_rect()`): a gun lying on its side is the silhouette of the painter's `flat` render (its side profile, `GunMesh.lying`, `flipped` the side `DroppedItem.flipped()` says is up, mirrored art) lifted as thick as the gun is wide (`art_size.x` times `thickness`, thinner at stock, guard, barrel: `lying_*`), its side profile on its top, tinted `lying_brighten` (1.4); a grenade is its body with the lever gone (`GrenadeArt`), a spoon its strip lifted `spoon_height` from its own art (`SpoonArt`); null shows nothing, which is also how an item that is never dropped, a magazine or a plate, stays unshown. It is laid about the middle of its footprint, its underside on the floor. The mirror places the node at the 2D node's place; the mesh's pivot turns it along `heading()` (`PI/2 - angle`: the muzzle `(cos a, sin a)` of 2D is `(cos a, 0, sin a)`) and lifts it by `altitude()` px (the fall, the lob's arc, 0 lying). Both nodes emit `shifted` while they move and the mesh writes its pivot on that alone, so a lying item costs the mesh nothing. A figure for the pixel post, as the held item. Tests: `tests/items/dropped_item_mesh_test.gd`, `thrown_grenade_mesh_test.gd`; windowed pictures `items_projectile_mesh_windowed_test.gd` (`ITEMS_SHOTS=<dir>`, `ITEMS_PITCH=<degrees>`).

The painters keep their 1 px outline ring round each part in the flat art (the item's own edge at 5 to 12 px; the look gate judges it against the post outline); `bevel`, `cast_shadows` and `glare` are no-ops flat.
