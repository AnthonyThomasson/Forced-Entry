# Character body: the 3D mesh (`body/art/body_mesh.gd`, `character_meshes.gd`, `CharacterMeshConfig`)

In the 3D render (#193) the mirror shows a living character by `BodyMesh`, a low-poly body of Art's mesh kit (`domain-art/meshes.md`), in place of the proxy capsule; `CharacterMeshes.register(builders)` puts its builder under the `CHARACTER` hook key and Main's `_register_meshes()` calls it. It reads what the painters read, never a copy of it: the **joints** are `anatomy.rig()` (solved once per physics frame and shared with the 2D draw, so the body faces `pose`'s angles, holds what the hands hold, strides as the gait says and folds in a crouch by `CharacterPostureConfig`: P3), the **colours** are `visuals.dress` (shirt, pants, shoes, skin, each arm segment, each glove) and its resolved look (`PixelArt.look_of(dress.head_opts)`: hair, cap or helmet, vest and plate colours, a pack). The parts are shared unit solids (a cylinder, a ball, a box through `MeshCache`) scaled and placed between the joints by `mirror_update(body)`, written only when a joint moved; a new look (`dress.look_key`) re-tints and shows or hides the optional parts (vest, plates, pack, hair, headwear, a boot's shaft) in place, no rebuild. The rig's heights are the drawing's (a head 100 px up), so they are squeezed to `CharacterMeshConfig.height` (58, the how-to's standing character); the horizontal reach is the 2D body's. A seated body (shapes disabled) is hidden by the mirror's own rule; a dead one goes with its death (no `on_body_died`: the ragdoll and the held item are later jobs of their own). Tests: `tests/character/character_mesh_test.gd` (the seams), `character_mesh_windowed_test.gd` (`CHARMESH_SHOTS=<dir>` saves the pictures; windowed only).
