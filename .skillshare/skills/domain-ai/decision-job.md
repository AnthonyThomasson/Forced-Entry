# AI · The decision job and its budget (`decision_job.gd`, `decision_budget.gd`)

THINK, one decision as a job: what claims a decision, builds its snapshot, refuses a held order, searches for a throw and walks the tree, piece by piece against a pool of work units shared by every NPC. The tree and the planner are `decision.md`; the build is `snapshot-staged.md`; the controller that asks is `orchestrator.md`.

## The pool (`DecisionBudget`)

NPCs that spawned or came under fire together want to decide on one frame. `per_frame` work units (default 1) a physics frame are shared by every NPC, counted, never timed, so when an NPC decides never depends on the machine, and two NPCs that would claim the same flank side decide on different frames, each hearing the other's radio first (`decisions_spread_over_frames`). A **piece** has a static price: `cost_start` (the build's START, the claim) 1, `cost_hostile`, `cost_finish`, `cost_refusal`, `cost_level` 0 and the throw piece's `cost_throw_block`, `cost_throw_rank`, `cost_throw_route` 0, so one decision is one unit and runs whole in its claim frame, each later level as Von answers. `take(cost, owed)` spends it when `spent + cost + owed` fits; a frame whose pool is untouched and owes nothing runs the next piece even if dearer than the pool (a job never stalls, nobody else gets that frame); a free piece always runs. Every build piece kind is priced explicitly (`cost_of`; -1 for a kind with no price, which the job reports with `push_error` and runs as free, so a piece added without a price is found at once and never costs a start).

**Who is served first** (`DecisionRoster`, `decision_roster.gd`). NPCs take their turns in the scene tree's order, so the pool is served in the order of the claims instead: each job in flight notes what its next piece costs as its turn ends, and a job asking the pool for a piece sets aside what the older jobs that have not had their turn this frame still need (`owed`). A new start is younger than every job in flight, so it runs only on what they leave; and a job is never overtaken by a younger one, so the one that waits longest waits only for the jobs older than itself (the starvation bound). A job not served for three frames (its NPC gone) owes nothing, and an NPC that stops thinking says so at once: `release()` (the character's `died`: a dead body no longer calls its controller; or leaving the tree) takes the job off the roster, `rest()` (its tick held by a service interaction) notes cost 0 for the frame; a job the roster had forgotten that runs again takes its place anew as the youngest claim. **Known limit:** the starts are in the scene tree's order (the NPCs ask in the tree's order and the frame's one start goes to the first that asks, each wish asked again every frame), so with many wishes at once an NPC late in the tree starts after those before it; the roster orders the pool among jobs in flight, not the starts. At most one decision *begins* a physics frame (`may_start`) whatever `per_frame` holds. At the default prices nothing but the claim costs, so none of this changes a frame. Tests set the statics with `set_static`; a native suite stands in for the frame with `frame_source` and ends with `reset()`. `DecisionRoster.claims()` counts decisions begun since `reset()` (the perf report reads it).

## The job (`DecisionJob`, preloaded by the controller, no `class_name`)

A data part holding no owner (ADR 0006): the perception, the planner and the cadence, and three `Callable` reads. `request(forced)` claims the START: refused, nothing changes and the controller's wish (`forced`, its reason, the elapsed decide timer) stands for next frame; claimed, any job in flight (building or walking) is dropped without a signal, and a cadence wish (`forced` false) never claims over one. The controller books the decision at the claim (`decisions`, the reason, the timer, `_apply_config`). `advance()` runs the pieces the pool allows, each frame, and is never run inside itself (a transport that answers at once asks for another round, which runs when this one ends): START (reads the goal, activity and engaged contact, the order held and the entry the decision starts from, then freezes the knowledge and the confinement copy), a HOSTILE piece per analysed hostile, FINISH, the **refusal step**, the **throw piece**, then the planner's levels. The refusal step ends the order the build saw and offered no option for (`ordered` false) and replaces the snapshot's confinement with the live one refreshed (`refresh`, read after the build); an order taken meanwhile is not judged on a snapshot made without it. The **throw piece** resolves the `grenade_ready` fact into the snapshot's facts (`SnapshotDemand.throw_search`, `snapshot-demand.md`) in steps of `throw_block` raw candidates, one rank pass or one route, each priced (`cost_throw_*`), so the planner finds the fact resolved and the search is not one frame's heavy lump; at the default prices it runs whole before the walk begins. As the throw piece ends, the last things before the walk, the radio callouts are read again and what the snapshot made of them is made again (`AgentPerception.reread_claims`: who holds each flank side, the flank options and sections, the allies on a hostile, the stack's stun), so a teammate's claim that landed during a sliced build or a priced throw search is seen and two NPCs on one target take different sides (`claim_seen_during_throw_piece`); then whether the behaviour's last choice is still running (`ongoing`, judged for "(your current plan)") is read, not at the claim. The walked-route memo (`hold_walks`) is held only while pieces run together in one `advance`, never between frames. `last_facts`, `last_sections`, `last_demand` and `last_analysed` (the cadence's) change only as a build completes: a dropped half-built job leaves them.

The planner is `driven`: `begin` queues its first level, `step()` runs one piece (follow a pick, decide a level), and an answer from Von queues the next and emits `piece_ready`, which the job answers with `advance()`: run at once if its price fits, else on a later `advance`. `decided` / `failed` are unchanged.

## Interrupting

The controller applies one test to a job in flight (`_maybe_interrupt`): an order taken since (`order_from`) restarts a job begun before it (`overtaken_by_order`: `decisions <= order_from`, as `_on_decided` judges a landed one), never one begun after it; else `stale(entry_for(quick_facts))`. An event during a build is not weighed by that build (`frozen()`: its wish is kept until the walk begins). A walk is stale when it started from another node, a build when its claim-time facts started from another. Stale, the forced decision claims over it (and is refused, the job carries on and the wish stays). Not stale, a walk is left to finish and the wish is dropped; a build whose START has run (`frozen()`) has not seen what forced it, so its wish is kept until the walk begins.

Tests: `ai/decision/decision_job_suite` (native: a job that finishes, an interrupt over a half-built job, a refused claim, an over-pool piece, the frozen snapshot, the refusal, no re-entry, an order overtaking only an older job, every piece kind priced), `decision_pool_suite` (one start a frame, the pool served oldest claim first: in-flight before a new start, no overtaking), `decision_job_pieces_suite` (the throw piece's steps priced, the callouts read again as it ends, the memo held per frame, the pool not run again to price the next step), `throw_piece_matches_whole` (the stepped search equals the one-shot at several step sizes), `claim_seen_as_the_walk_begins` (a claim heard during a build is in the snapshot), `claim_seen_during_throw_piece` (one radioed during a priced throw piece is too), `dead_npc_frees_the_pool` / `freed_npc_frees_the_pool` (an NPC dead or freed mid-build holds no one up: the next claims on the very next frame), `decision_pieces_over_frames` (priced pieces end to end; a decision forced mid-build is kept), `decision_refused_claim_keeps_wish` (the controller keeps a refused wish), `decisions_spread_over_frames`.
