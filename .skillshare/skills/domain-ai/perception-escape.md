# AI · Perception · The escape pool (`escape_pool.gd`, `escape_search.gd`)

Side file of `perception-grenades.md` (the plan, the zone, the safe spot). Spec: `docs/grenades.md` G16.

One escape search cost 2 to 8.5 ms (a snap about 22 us, a verdict about 28, a route about 125, the candidates round a burst 300 to 600), the frames of a grenade in flight in the opening's tail. A search is a job (`EscapeSearch`: the candidates in their order, the best so far, resumable) against `EscapePool.per_frame` **80 units** a physics frame across every NPC (about 2 ms; a snap 1, a verdict 1, a route 5, the candidates round a burst 16 once: counts of work done, never a clock). `AgentGrenades._begin` makes the search and registers it; `_landed` is its plan once finished. A search is served on every ask, the frame after it began, in this order (a rule, `EscapePool._before`): first an **urgent** search, the one whose grenade goes off first of them, then the one begun first (its wave stays in the order below); then a plan with none yet before a replan, the grenade that goes off first, the NPC nearest the burst, the search begun first. A search is urgent when it began `AGE_FRAMES` (12) ago or more, or its grenade goes off within `ARRIVE_MARGIN` and `AGE_FRAMES` (36 frames: `EscapePool.urgent`). **The bound on a wait** (for an owner asking at least every `STALE_FRAMES`, 30: a search left unasked is dropped): a search lands by its deadline (its grenade's fuse frame less `ARRIVE_MARGIN`) whenever the work of the searches whose fuse is no later than its own fits in the time, the sooner fuse first once urgent (under overload nothing is promised: a search past its deadline has the soonest fuse, so it heads the urgent class ahead of ones that could still make theirs; a fuse frame is the fuse left rounded to frames at begin, so two NPCs on one grenade can be 1 frame apart and are ordered as two fuses); among searches of one fuse, or a later fuse than those before it, within `AGE_FRAMES` plus the work of every search begun before it or on its frame, over the pool, whatever arrives after. **The trade of the deadline order**: a far search with a long fuse waits behind searches begun after it with a sooner fuse, so behind a stream of them it has only its deadline for a bound, not 12 frames (`escape_pool_bounds_wait`: 2 searches a frame of a 0.5 s fuse for 50 frames held the far search 60 of its 66 frames, the first-begun order 12), and a frag thrown 20 frames after a stun wave of 60 (fuse 42 frames) is planned within 9 frames, 18 allowed, where the first-begun order made it wait 28 behind the wave; both pinned there, as are 10 new grenades a frame (each paying its 16 units: they take nothing from the urgent work) and a young replan whose fuse is 30 frames overtaking 30 older new plans (the fuse clause of `urgent`). The pool is work-conserving, and while any search is urgent a frame holds `EscapePool.urgent_extra` (80) more units, twice the rate (a frame of urgent work serves up to 167 units, about 4 ms, and 87 while none is urgent, both pinned by a literal; a new burst's 16 units come on top, `peak_spent` is served plus built, `peak_served` the served): a wave of N searches of about 110 units (the mean of a new plan over the ai domain's tests: median 65, p90 232, dearest 620) lands within 12 frames at the pool's rate and then at twice it, N x 0.7 frames, so its last NPC is served before the shortest fuse (a stun, 1.5 s = 90 frames) less the margin (66) up to about 80 NPCs at once. A search that cannot finish continues next frame; the NPC carries on as it was (no plan yet, or its old one). Its plan is from where the NPC stood and the fuse left when the search began (`from`, `began_fuse` in the plan): it lands `w` frames later than it was made for, and `ARRIVE_MARGIN` (0.4 s, 24 frames) is what absorbs that. Thirty defenders and four more round one frag (`escape_pool_spreads_plans`, real searches) waited up to 19 frames at the pool alone, and one of them (a 300 px run planned to arrive 0.4 s before the burst, begun 19 frames late) was caught; with the urgent extra the most is 15 frames (16 with the beat) and none is. (Planning for the fuse left at landing, by shrinking the run allowed with the search's age, was tried and dropped: the shortest route is the winner whatever the allowance, so it only turned a late runner into one with no spot, who crouches where the burst finds it: 10 of the 34 caught.) A frame serves the pool, the urgent extra and at most one candidate more (167 units), and a new burst's candidates cost their 16 units when the search is begun, taking nothing from the searches waiting (`EscapePool.peak_served` is the first, `peak_spent` both: 167 plus 16 per new burst). The order is made once a frame and a call after the frame's units are served returns at once. A search unasked for `STALE_FRAMES` (30) is dropped, and is not served again if asked about (`serving`: its NPC begins a new one from where it stands with the fuse left); one whose NPC has died or been freed is cancelled (`EscapeSearch.alive`, set by `AgentGrenades._begin`) and spends none of the pool; outside a physics frame a search runs whole. Latency, search begun to plan, median / p90 / max frames, 271 pooled searches of 40 ai tests with the 34-NPC wave: **all searches** (replans included) 3 / 19 / 21; **fresh plans alone** 2 / 14 / 16. The 34 plan within 16 of knowing the frag (`WITHIN_FRAMES` 16 is the measured value: 15, and a frame more for the half off their beat); before the pool a plan landed the frame it began. Against the shortest fuse (90 frames) the max is 23 %. `has_escape` is not pooled; a stack member whose slot is its cover (`Entry.expects_burst`) still plans.
