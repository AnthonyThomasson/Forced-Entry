Why these: the pool is sized in milliseconds, so a piece is priced at what it measured, rounded up for a piece that costs a measurable share of a millisecond and left free when it does not. The level was tried at 1 unit (a decision then needs 3 units for 0.3 ms): the median decision took 5 frames, not 2 (one run each, a different fight each), for a tail of decision work that fell only from 12.9 to 7.5 ms. The throw block is a 48-candidate step (three of the 16-candidate steps a measured 0.13 to 0.3 ms each) so the heavy decile of searches, 7 to 10 ms in a grenade fight, runs 8 to 12 units over several frames instead of in one. The planner level that first built a lazy group (`leads` 2.3 ms mean and 7.4 max, a hostile's `grenade_throws` 0.7 mean and 4.8 max, `entries` 1.6) is now sliced by the group and grenade pieces (`decision-groups.md`), a unit a step at one unit, which rounds a 0.3 to 1.2 ms step up as the rest of the table does (one run each of the 10v10 and grenade matches, load 4 to 7, throwaway timers, never committed: few samples, so the unit is a rounding, not a fit).

Firings (one run each, 10v10 stress and grenade match, the whole test): 44 and 15 re-reads, the leads and entries built ahead in one (10v10, frame 1071), none forgotten: a match decision starts below the root, so the group piece rarely runs in these guards (`SnapshotCounts` `forgotten_entries` / `forgotten_leads` count it).

**Observing a decision's landing.** A decision claims at once (`decisions` grows at the claim) and lands frames later, so a test or a probe that reads its result waits for `debug_state()["decisions"]` past the count it saw and `debug_state()["decision"]["pending"]` (also `debug_brain()["pending"]`) false: `pending` is the job's (claimed, building, searching for a throw or walking), not only the planner's walk, so it holds through the build too. Tests that carry the prices as a premise name them: `tests/_ai/job_fakes.gd` has `whole_prices()` (a pool of one, every piece but the claim free: the decision runs whole in one frame) and `prices()` / `set_prices()` to put the game's own back. `sliced_matches_whole` (the shipped prices and the whole ones decide the same on a still world: levels, path, facts, sections) and `sliced_flank_sides_differ` (two NPCs on one cadence and one target take different sides under the shipped prices; the re-read itself is pinned by `claim_seen_during_throw_piece` and `claim_seen_as_the_walk_begins`) are the scenario guards; a scenario test never pins the whole prices to dodge the latency.
