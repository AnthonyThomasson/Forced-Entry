# The 3D camera rig (`scenes/general/camera_rig.gd`)

Side file of `domain-general`.

`CameraRig` (`camera_rig.gd`, interface 27) is the 3D view's camera: an orthographic `Camera3D` that frames what the sim's `Camera2D` frames. The
`Camera2D` stays the focus, zoom and shake source (Sound, the cull and the tests read it); the rig reads its `get_screen_center_position()` (focus
plus shake offset) and `zoom` each frame and places the camera on the ground point (x, 0, y) at `RenderConfig.pitch_degrees` (80, near top-down: the 2D view with a thin sliver of side, the ground foreshortened by sin 80) and
its `yaw` (a property in degrees, 0 to start: north up; setting it also sets the turn's target). **Q / E turn it a quarter** (`_turn`: a *press* of `Keybinds.get_camera_turn()` adds `CameraTurnConfig.step_degrees`, 90, to the target; the yaw eases to it (`speed_degrees`, `ease_rate`, `min_speed_degrees`: about 0.4 s a quarter) and ends exactly on it, wrapped to [0, 360), so it rests only on a cardinal; presses queue, holding does not repeat; `turning()`). The sim's `CameraController` follows `follow_point()`: the followed character's 2D place plus how far the drawn vehicle it rides leads its 2D footprint (a car's mesh is on its 3D twin, a physics step ahead: 21 px at 1269 px/s). The rig binds `Pointer` to its camera and displayed rect in `bind`, and runs at `process_priority` 50, before the player's view (100) and every `GroundLayer` (110), which read it. It sizes the `SubViewport` it is bound to (`RenderConfig.texel_match`: a picture pixel is `round(zoom)` screen pixels and covers `art_pixel` 2D px, so at a whole zoom one pixel of a 2D painter's art is one picture pixel times the zoom, its texels crisp; a zoom below 1 covers several art pixels; with the rows and columns that cover the window, times `oversample`, plus a 1 px margin) and snaps the focus to that pixel grid, shifting the shown rectangle back by the remainder, so a following
camera does not shimmer. `RenderConfig` holds `unit` (the one 2D-to-3D scale, Art's mirror reads it too), the camera and the view's canvas layer;
`RenderLightConfig` the 3D view's backdrop colour (the lights are the Lighting domain's, `WorldLighting3D`). In the 3D render `CameraController` does not ease its zoom: it holds one of `Zoom3dConfig.steps` (`ZoomSteps`, `zoom_steps.gd`: the wheel steps off the automatic zoom's step, the framing takes the largest step not over its fit, a vehicle's zoom-out the nearest) so the rig's texel match holds. `RenderConfig.art_pixel` mirrors `ArtCanvasConfig.pixel_size` (General cannot read Art; a test pins them equal); `Lighting3DConfig.sun_elevation` puts a flat top in the toon light's top band and a wall's shadow short (`render_light_top_in_base_band_test.gd`). Tests: `camera_rig_turns_in_quarters_test.gd`, `camera_zoom_steps_3d_test.gd`, `zoom_steps_rules_test.gd`, `camera_zoom_vehicle_3d_test.gd`, windowed `render_3d_camera_shots_windowed_test.gd` (`CAMERA_SHOT_DIR`). `CameraController.focus_targets()` names who the view is about (the player, or every combatant spectating) for Art's wall fade (interface 27). Test: `tests/general/camera_rig_frames_focus_test.gd`.


## The flat overlays (`ground_layer.gd`)

`GroundLayer` (a `CanvasLayer`, `class_name`) is where the 3D render draws what the hidden 2D world cannot: the debug overlays. `ViewLayers.overlay(item)` calls `GroundLayer.adopt(item)` (in a world with no 3D view the item waits in the group `ground_overlay_waiting`, and `GroundLayer.shared()` adopts the group when the layer is made: a test world's overlays, made at its build, before the session's view), which, once the item is in the tree and the frame's tree changes are over, moves it under a ground layer. **One shared layer** (`GroundLayer.overlays`, made by the 3D view with `GroundLayer.shared()`, on `RenderConfig.overlay_layer`) holds every debug overlay of the match, one plain `Node` per owner (`For<id>`) so two owners' `VisionDebug` keep their names; each frame it puts each overlay on its owner's global transform (an overlay draws from its owner's origin and turns with it, as in 2D), frees them when the owner leaves the tree, and `GroundLayer.overlays_of(owner)` lists them. The player's view is no overlay in 3D (two masks the toon shaders read: `domain-vision`, V12). A `CanvasLayer` is not a `CanvasItem`, so the hidden world does not hide what is under it, and the layer's `transform` is `Pointer.screen_transform` each frame (priority 110, after the rig and the view), so the overlay's world-space drawing lies flat on the 3D floor at any yaw. In 2D it does nothing. Tests: `tests/general/ground_overlays_3d_test.gd` (Main whole, a played 3D match: one shared layer), `render_3d_turn_windowed_test.gd` (windowed). The player's view keeps running while the tree is paused in 3D (a menu pauses it and the rig still follows the camera: `tests/general/view_follows_paused_camera_3d_test.gd`).
