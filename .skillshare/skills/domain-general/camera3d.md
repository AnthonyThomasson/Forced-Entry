# The 3D camera rig (`scenes/general/camera_rig.gd`)

Side file of `domain-general`.

`CameraRig` (`camera_rig.gd`, interface 27) is the 3D view's camera: an orthographic `Camera3D` that frames what the sim's `Camera2D` frames. The
`Camera2D` stays the focus, zoom and shake source (Sound, the cull and the tests read it); the rig reads its `get_screen_center_position()` (focus
plus shake offset) and `zoom` each frame and places the camera on the ground point (x, 0, y) at `RenderConfig.pitch_degrees` (35.264, true isometric) and
its `yaw` (a property in degrees, 45 to start). **Q / E turn it** (`_turn`: while `Keybinds.get_camera_turn()` is held the yaw turns at `RenderConfig.turn_speed_degrees`, 90 a second, free of any snap and about the focus; none held, it stays). The rig binds `Pointer` to its camera and displayed rect in `bind`, and runs at `process_priority` 50, before the player's view (100) and every `GroundLayer` (110), which read it. It sizes the `SubViewport` it is bound to (`RenderConfig.internal_lines`
rows at a `reference_height` window: a picture pixel is `reference_height / internal_lines` screen pixels rounded to a whole number, 3, at any window size, with the rows that cover the window, times `oversample`, plus a 1 px margin) and snaps the focus to that pixel grid, shifting the shown rectangle back by the remainder, so a following
camera does not shimmer. `RenderConfig` holds `unit` (the one 2D-to-3D scale, Art's mirror reads it too), the camera and the view's canvas layer;
`RenderLightConfig` the interim sun and ambient. `CameraController.focus_targets()` names who the view is about (the player, or every combatant spectating) for Art's wall fade (interface 27). Test: `tests/general/camera_rig_frames_focus_test.gd`.


## The flat overlays (`ground_layer.gd`)

`GroundLayer` (a `CanvasLayer`, `class_name`) is where the 3D render mode draws what the hidden 2D world cannot: the player's view (its composite) and the debug overlays. While `GroundLayer.enabled` (Main's 3D view sets it) `ViewLayers.overlay(item)` and the view's composite call `GroundLayer.adopt(item, canvas_layer)`, which, once the item is in the tree and the frame's tree changes are over, moves it under a ground layer. **One shared layer** (`GroundLayer.overlays`, made by the 3D view with `GroundLayer.shared()`, on `RenderConfig.overlay_layer`) holds every debug overlay of the match, one plain `Node` per owner (`For<id>`) so two owners' `VisionDebug` keep their names; each frame it puts each overlay on its owner's global transform (an overlay draws from its owner's origin and turns with it, as in 2D), frees them when the owner leaves the tree, and `GroundLayer.overlays_of(owner)` lists them. The composite, one to a match, sits under a `GroundVision` layer beside it (`vision_layer`) that dies with the view. A `CanvasLayer` is not a `CanvasItem`, so the hidden world does not hide what is under it, and the layer's `transform` is `Pointer.screen_transform` each frame (priority 110, after the rig and the view), so the overlay's world-space drawing lies flat on the 3D floor at any yaw. In 2D it does nothing. Tests: `tests/general/ground_overlays_3d_test.gd` (Main whole, a played 3D match: one shared layer), `render_3d_turn_windowed_test.gd` (windowed). The composite and the player's view keep running while the tree is paused in 3D (a menu pauses it and the rig still turns: `tests/general/view_follows_paused_turn_3d_test.gd`). There the memory capture is off, so the composite dims the unseen ground by `RenderConfig.unseen_dim` (0.6; 1 is the 2D black), read when the composite is built (a change applies to the next match), instead of blacking it; `tests/vision/composite_dims_unseen_in_3d_test.gd`.
