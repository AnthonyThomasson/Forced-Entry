# General: body heights and plan space (`scenes/general/`)

Side file of `domain-general`; signatures are architecture interface 11.

## BodyHeights

`BodyHeights` (`body_heights.gd`, statics, interface 11) is the one holder of a body's real proportions: 1 px is a 3D unit, a 1.75 m person is 74 px, a metre is 42.3 px (`px_per_metre()`, `px`, `metres`). Each posture (`STAND`, `DUCK`, `FIRE_OVER`) has its `head_top`, `eye`, `muzzle` (the bore line), `shoulder` and four hit-zone bands (`zones`, `zone_at`: `feet`, `legs`, `torso`, `head`) in px above the floor, from `BodyHeights.postures` (metres; the research and the reasoning for each number are `docs/research/3d-cover-heights.md`, section 7). A duck is the low pose behind cover (head top 0.85 m: hidden by a piece at least that tall); fire-over is the pose it rises to for a burst when its muzzle line is blocked (bore 1.02 m, over 0.9 m cover, head and shoulders showing). **The rise to fire** (D7a): `lowest_clearing(top) -> StringName` is the lowest posture whose muzzle clears a piece of cover `top` px tall, so a ducked body rises only as far as it must: the duck itself under its own muzzle (0.7 m), `FIRE_OVER` for cover up to its muzzle (1.02 m), `STAND` for taller cover up to a standing muzzle (1.55 m), `BodyHeights.NONE` (`&""`) above that: no fire-over, it fires around the cover. A muzzle level with the top does not clear it. The 0 to 100 coverage roll still decides shots; this holds the numbers the real-cover jobs read. The 3D mirror's character is its standing head top (`MirrorConfig.character_height` is `BodyHeights.person_px`). The drawn crouch (0.62 of 74, 45.9 px, `CharacterPostureConfig`) and `DUCK`'s 36 are two numbers for now: they converge when the duck posture lands (the `duck-rise` job). Test: `tests/general/body_heights_test.gd`.

## PlanSpace

`PlanSpace` (`plan_space.gd`, statics, interface 11) lifts a plan-space `Vector2` (the floor plan `(x, y)` the AI, world generation, navigation and most tests use, `(x, z)` on the 3D ground) and a height in px to a `Vector3` `(x, height, y) * RenderConfig.unit` (`lift`), and takes one back (`plan_of`, `height_of`). The mirror's lift (`MirrorProxy.lift`, a floor slab) and the camera rig's ground point use it, so the mapping is written once. `lift_transform(xf, height := 0.0)` lifts a body's 2D transform to its 3D one (the 2D turn `a` becomes `-a` about up; a local `(x, y)` is the local `(x, 0, y)`, so a shape's offset in its body survives) and `plan_transform(xf3)` takes it back: what the twin space places its twins by (`twins.md`). Test: `tests/general/plan_space_test.gd`.
