# Research: who gives the go when a stacked team enters a room

Primary-source research for the leader's go at a held stack (`docs/leadership.md` LD13 / LD14, `docs/tactical-entry.md`).
It covers how a four-person military fire team or police entry team stacked on a doorway is released into the room:
who signals, what the stack does while it waits, how the entry method is chosen, and what happens without the leader.

## 1. Who gives the go

- **The team leader is #2 and controls the entry.** "The members of the fire team are assigned numbers 1 through 4,
  with the team leader normally designated number 2." (FM 3-06.11 ¶3-20d, p. 3-26.) "Upon the team leader's signal
  to enter the room (or upon the explosion of the hand grenade), the clearing team moves quickly through the door."
  (ATTP 3-06.11 ¶7-111, p. 7-27.) For a close-in breach "the team leader gives a nonverbal signal to execute the
  breach", and the breach itself is the go (¶7-112, ¶7-84: "Executing a close-in breach is the signal to begin entering").
- **Police: #2 squeezes #1.** "The #2-man controls the movement into the next room"; "#2-man initiates entry by
  squeezing #1-man" (JLE 140S lab I.A.5, I.B.1).
- **Readiness runs forward from the rear.** "All team members must signal one another that they are ready before the
  team moves to the entry point" and "avoid the use of verbal signals" (FM 3-06.11 ¶3-22c(3)(b)–(c), p. 3-29). In a
  police hallway team, "The left flanker squeezes the right flanker to let him know he is ready", then "The right
  flanker squeezes the point man indicating that he should now move past the door" (Papenfuhs). The Army's verbal
  equivalent is "UP: Given by an individual to report they are ready to continue the mission" (ATTP Table 7-1, p. 7-2).
- **The point can hold the go.** In the USMC two-man split stack, after both have scanned from the doorway, "Shooter
  Number Two stands up and yells, 'READY,' and Shooter Number One Yells, 'MOVE.' Both shooters enter the room
  together" (MCWP 3-35.3 app. A ¶20c–d, pp. A-32–A-33). The police rule "The #1-man is always right (correct)"
  (JLE 140S I.A.1) gives the point the say on direction once moving.
- **Leader outside the stack.** In the Army foothold drill the squad leader is not in the stack: "The squad leader
  positions himself where he can best control his teams", then commands "COOK OFF GRENADES" or "PREPARE TO
  ENTER—GO!", and "Upon detonation of both grenades (or command GO), the buddy team flows into the room" (FM 3-21.8
  ¶7-190, pp. 7-39–7-40). In police deliberate entries "mandate that the team leader doesn't make entry into any
  rooms"; team leaders find "5th in the stack works out best" (French). The USMC covering team "is positioned by the
  assault element leader" outside the room (MCWP app. A ¶19).
- **What changes with the leader in or out.** In the stack, the leader is #2 and his squeeze or his own move is the go.
  Behind it, he commands the go (verbally if surprise is gone) and does not enter; the stack still self-synchronises
  on the first man's movement and the grenade's detonation.

## 2. Holding at the door

- **Ideally there is no hold.** "Ideally, the team arrives and passes through the entry point without having to stop."
  "Unless a grenade is being thrown prior to entry, the team should avoid stopping outside the point of entry."
  (FM 3-06.11 ¶3-21, p. 3-27; ¶3-22c(4), p. 3-29.) When it must stop "to line up or to throw a hand grenade … they
  should do so only momentarily and … ensure they have sufficient cover" (ATTP ¶7-115). Police name the deliberate
  version: "Breach and hold … maintain and hold their positions in close proximity of the entry point … after
  breaching" (NV POST glossary, p. 36).
- **No crossing before the go.** Team members arrive "prepared to enter on a single command" (FM 3-06.11 ¶3-18,
  p. 3-24); they move "on the signal to go, or immediately after the grenade detonates" (¶3-22c(5)). No source lets a
  stacked member enter on his own.
- **The stack is a target.** "Caution should be taken when using a stacked type of position as personnel may be
  concentrating in a danger area where they could be exposed to fires penetrating walls or explosive effects from
  boobytraps or grenades thrown by the enemy" (MCWP app. A sec. V, p. A-27). The door is "the fatal funnel" (FM 3-06.11
  ¶3-21a). Stacks "should be very tight" (ATTP ¶7-96).
- **Security while holding.** "Maintain security … Do not neglect rear security" (FM 3-06.11 ¶3-18). For an explosive
  breach, "The number 1 man provides security at the doorway … The number 3 man provides security overhead, and the
  number 4 man provides rear security" (¶3-20d(2)). Police: "there must be guns covering all threats all the time"
  (French); the point and rear guard stay in the hall "looking in opposite directions" (Papenfuhs).
- **Fighting from the slots is doctrine.** USMC split stack, no grenade: "the stand/kneel positioning of shooters places
  the shooters' weapons at different levels, thus allowing each shooter to engage targets inside the room while
  staying out of the other shooter's line of fire. The shooters scan the room for targets" (MCWP app. A ¶20b, fig. A-38
  "Shooters Clear Sectors of Fire From Doorway"). An Army team holding beside an enemy-occupied room: "Soldier 4
  remained covering the open doorway; the other three Soldiers in order to not cross in front of the open doorway
  assumed ready positions beside the side of the door" (ATTP ¶7-164, p. 7-39). The foothold drill keeps one buddy team
  "short of the entry point to add suppressive fires for the initial entry" (FM 3-21.8 ¶7-190). Pieing: "As soon as a
  hostile threat is seen, immediate, accurate fire can be placed on the threat" (MCWP app. A ¶3a).
- **After engaging: enter or hold, on the leader's call.** In the USMC drill the scan and any fire from the doorway come
  first, then #1's "MOVE" takes both in (MCWP ¶20c–d). Police deliberate and limited-penetration methods may never
  enter: "you can clear objectives from the outside before making the actual entry", and "you can attempt to verbalize
  commands to have them crawl out of the room" (French); "Limited penetration: … a measured and partial entry … Suspects
  … can be detained at the point of the entry and/or be called out" (NV POST, p. 38); BART teaches "Threshold
  Evaluation" and "Visual Clearing Techniques" as alternatives (BART II.9). The immediate threats doctrine names include
  enemy "within 3 to 5 feet of the breach point" (FM 3-06.11 ¶3-21c).

## 3. Choosing and signalling the entry method

- **Chosen before the door, by the leader.** "Typically, a squad leader or higher finalizes the plan for clearing a room",
  which "Dictates the assault conditions (surgical, precision, or high intensity)" (ATTP ¶7-86, p. 7-22). "Determine
  whether to throw a hand grenade, and the type based on likely occupants and wall characteristics … Once the decisions
  on breaching and hand grenade use are finalized, the clearing team … can move into position." (¶7-108, p. 7-26.)
- **Frag is for high-intensity conditions only.** High intensity: "employ fragmentation grenades to neutralize an area
  before entering"; precision: "retains the option of employing hand grenades if called for" (ATTP ¶7-140–7-141).
  FM 3-06.11 lists "fragmentation, concussion, stun" and requires use "consistent with the ROE and building structure"
  (¶3-22c(4)(a)–(b)); frags only "where … the presence of noncombatants is highly unlikely" (NOTE after ¶3-22c(5)). The
  USMC warns M67 frag fragments "may injure Marines outside the room" and prefers concussion grenades (MCWP ¶19c).
  Police use only noise-flash devices, "when warranted" (French).
- **At the door: the leader signals, #2 throws, the bang is the go.** "If a hand grenade is to be thrown, it is readied by
  the number 2 man, and, on the team leader's nonverbal signal, the hand grenade is thrown into the room." (ATTP
  ¶7-110.) The thrower calls "FRAG OUT", "CONCUSSION OUT", "STUN OUT" or, for stealth, gives only a visual signal
  (FM 3-06.11 ¶3-22c(4)(c)). USMC: "a visual showing of the grenade is made to assault element members, and a visual
  acknowledgment from them is received" (MCWP ¶19c(2)). Police: "Show it to number 1-man prior to throwing … Peek into
  room before throwing" (JLE 140S I.B.6); "The user MUST confirm positively that all areas are safe prior to tossing
  the device" (Ijames). The team goes "immediately after the grenade detonates" (FM 3-06.11 ¶3-22c(5)).
- **The one drill where the go names the method.** FM 3-21.8 ¶7-190: the squad leader at the door either commands "COOK
  OFF GRENADES" or, "If the squad leader decides not to use grenades, he commands, PREPARE TO ENTER—GO!".
- **Quiet entry.** Police "Slow and deliberate: Movement technique characterized by stealth and without regard for time"
  (NV POST, p. 40); deliberate entries "slice the pie" before entry (French). Army "precision" uses "mechanical or other
  less destructive breaching methods" (ATTP ¶7-141).

## 4. The leader as point

- **"Follow me" is a movement rule, not an entry rule.** "The leader's standing order to his Soldiers is: 'Follow me and
  do as I do.' … When he fires, his Soldiers also fire." (FM 3-21.8 ¶3-35, p. 3-8, the fire-team wedge in the open.)
- **At a door, the first man moving releases the rest.** "The #1-man never enters into a room alone" (JLE 140S I.A.2).
  "The number 2 Soldier reads the number 1 Soldier's movement and goes in the opposite direction" (ATTP ¶7-98); #2
  "enter[s] almost simultaneously with the first" (FM 3-06.11 ¶3-22c(6)). After the bang, "Shooter Number Two follows
  immediately behind Shooter Number One" (MCWP ¶19d). So "the point crosses = go" is recognised whoever the point is;
  doctrine simply puts the leader at #2, not #1.
- **A point may go in alone and call the others in.** "If Shooter Number One … enters a room without Shooter Number Two
  immediately following him … He then gives the command to Shooter Number Two, 'NEXT MAN IN, LEFT (RIGHT).'" Once in,
  "the clearing team leader can call in additional members with the 'NEXT MAN IN' command" (MCWP ¶19g, p. A-31).
  "SHORT ROOM" from #1 or #2 keeps #3 and #4 outside (ATTP ¶7-119, Table 7-1).

## 5. When the leader leaves or falls

- **No source gives a drill for a stack whose leader is gone.** What doctrine does say:
- **The controller is a slot.** After each room the police team re-forms so that the third man "moves up to become the
  #2-man into the next room" (JLE 140S I.B.3.i): whoever is #2 controls the next entry. "The entry order should always
  remain the same; however, the Soldiers can switch positions and, therefore, numbers as METT-TC dictates" (ATTP ¶7-164).
- **Succession.** The automatic rifleman "is normally the senior Soldier of the fire team" and must "Be prepared to assume
  the duties of the team leader" (FM 3-21.8 ¶1-76). "MAN DOWN: Given by an individual to inform other team members that
  a Soldier is down … and cannot continue the mission" (ATTP Table 7-1).
- **A casualty does not stop a running entry.** "If a Soldier becomes engaged in a hand-to-hand fight, the other Soldiers
  should continue their actions to dominate the room before rendering support" (ATTP ¶7-120); a man with a malfunction
  "must clear the doorway" (¶7-6). Readiness is mutual, not the leader's alone (FM 3-06.11 ¶3-22c(3)(b)).
- **On-scene authority.** Police "Compromise authority: Direct action authority granted by the Incident Command/Tactical
  Commander to a team leader authorizing the initiation of specific action … in response to actions taken by the
  suspect(s)" (NV POST, p. 37): the go when compromised sits with the team leader on scene, not a remote commander.

## Implications for the game

Against the rule "the stack holds at the threshold until the leader steps in or gives a go naming quiet / stun / frag;
held members fight from their slots, no crossing":

1. **Holding for a go matches doctrine; holding at all is the exception.** Every source waits for a signal, and none lets
   a stacked member cross early. But doctrine's default is no stop (FM 3-06.11 ¶3-21) and the stack is a target
   (MCWP sec. V). A long held stack is realistic only as police "breach and hold" or a covered start position.
2. **The method is normally chosen before the stack forms, not at the go.** ATTP ¶7-108 fixes grenade and type before
   moving into position. A go that names quiet / stun / frag matches one Army drill (FM 3-21.8 ¶7-190: "COOK OFF
   GRENADES" or "PREPARE TO ENTER—GO!") and is defensible, but the more common pattern is the mode on the stack order
   and a bare go at the door.
3. **The bang is the go.** For stun and frag, the leader's signal releases the throw (#2 throws) and the detonation sends
   the team in, with no second signal (ATTP ¶7-110–7-111). Frag only with no noncombatants likely and walls that
   stop fragments (ATTP ¶7-140; MCWP ¶19c).
4. **"Leader steps in = go" is consistent with doctrine** in the form "the first man moving releases the stack" (JLE
   140S "#1 never enters alone"; ATTP ¶7-98). Doctrine puts the leader at #2, giving the go by squeeze, not by stepping
   in first; a leader directing from outside commands the go and need not enter (FM 3-21.8 ¶7-190; French).
5. **Fighting from the slots is right, with limits.** Engaging what is seen through the doorway from the stack, with
   one member covering the opening and the rest beside the door out of the funnel, is doctrine (MCWP ¶20b; ATTP
   ¶7-164). Doctrine adds two things the rule lacks: rear and overhead security while held (FM 3-06.11 ¶3-20d(2)),
   and that after the doorway is clear of threats the leader may either send the team in or keep it outside (police
   limited penetration, call-outs).
6. **The go is collective readiness plus one controller.** Readiness flows forward ("UP", the squeeze from the last
   man); the controller (#2 / team leader) releases. A barrier on every member being ready before the go is realistic.
7. **Leader loss: doctrine points to the next senior taking the go, not an automatic one.** No source has a stack
   charge in because its leader fell; the #2 slot or the senior member inherits control (JLE 140S; FM 3-21.8 ¶1-76),
   and on compromise the on-scene team leader has authority to act (NV POST). An automatic go on the leader's death
   (LD D12) is a design choice, not doctrine; a closer model is that the stack's new controller decides (go or hold).
