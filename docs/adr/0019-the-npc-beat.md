# The NPC beat: an NPC senses and acts one frame in two (#194)

**Context.** After the decision pool and the exact cuts, the AI control floor was the cost left in the match frame guards: `movement.pull` 9.6 to 11 ms a frame over 20 NPCs (act 4.5, sense 4.35, think 2.0), and `perf/match_10v10_opening` p95 sat at 22 ms against a 20 ms standard. No exact cut reached it. The user chose a per-NPC cadence, knowing it moves AI recordings and frame pins and costs a reaction up to a frame (17 ms).

**Decision.** `scenes/ai/npc_pace.gd`. Each NPC runs its SENSE and ACT on a **beat**, one frame in `NpcPace.BEAT` (2), phase `(WorldRng.frame_of(world) + WorldRng.order_of(NPC)) % 2` (the world's frame and the NPC's spawn order, never a process count). A frame between beats is a coast: the body goes on with the intent the last beat wrote, a burst owing a round is fired, the decision job and the queued events run, and the coast's seconds are carried into the next beat's `delta`, so every timer counts real time. A hit on it, a grenade or explosion event, an order, blindness, and a throw, a tactical entry (its stack, pie, crossing, and its approach within 200 px of the doorway or as a guard's detour), a door being worked, an evade or a primed ambusher wake it at once. The rule, the wake list and why each item stays on its frame: `.skillshare/skills/domain-ai/beat.md`, `docs/gaze-and-fire.md` "The beat".

**Why the entry stays every frame.** First cut put only the doorway crossing (`committed`) on every frame. The guard and secure reads (a detour that covers a doorway the NPC walks past, `entry_guard_secures_passed_doorway`, `corridor_doors_not_passed`, `team_flows_in`, a stack waiting for all) then ran a beat apart from the walk they guard and covered the doorway after the invader was abreast of it (0.2 s covered of the 0.8 s the spec asks): a real behaviour change, so the scheduling was fixed, not the pin. Entries are a minority of NPC frames. A first wake list (forced decision, a known live grenade, the whole entry) kept 77 % of NPC frames on the beat (`control()` 9.1 to 8.3 ms a frame); the forced flag and the known grenade stay up for tens of frames, so only the events that start them wake the NPC (6.2 ms, 40 % coasts). Measured 700 frames from first contact of the seed 23 10v10, `control()` timed: 9.1 ms a frame with no beat, 8.3 with the first wake list, 6.2 with this one; 23 % of NPC frames coasted, then 40 %. The three wakes dropped: the forced-decision flag, a live grenade merely known (1500 NPC-frames of 12600) and the entry approach beyond 200 px of its doorway.

**Consequences.** A sighting is aimed at a frame later, the first shot of a fight a frame later, a held character is re-tested every 4 frames (3 before). Locomotion's waits count the beat's seconds (`loco.crowd.step`). Frame pins and the Von recordings of the AI tests moved; each was re-recorded with this cause. Measured cost, gain and reaction: the paragraphs below.

**Reaction on a fixed scenario (same fight both arms; one stationary invader, a hostile stepping from behind a fridge into view at 24 placements: four spots, four frame offsets so every beat phase is hit).** Frames from the step to the NPC seeing it and to its aim point within 40 px of it (the two are the same frame): 83999a4c always 2 (mean 1.75 to 2.0); the beat 1 to 4 (mean 2.5, median 3), so +0.75 frames on average and +2 at worst: the newly seen character is tested on the NPC's next beat (up to a frame), and the aim is written by the same beat. A hit reaches the NPC through the queued events, read every frame in both builds, and wakes its beat the frame it lands (`ai/pace/npc_beats_on_its_frames`): no added latency by construction. The first shot is paced by the NPC's own dwell, not the beat (the mean frame to first shot over 150 frames differed by 0.8 between builds).

**Merged with the escape pool (EscapePool).** `escape_pool_spreads_plans`: the half of the thirty off its beat on the frame the frag comes sees it on its next, so the thirty know it within two frames (15 and 15, not 30 on one), a plan is within 16 frames of knowing (15 before), and the nearest-first order is read among the NPCs that knew on one frame; `grenade_overlay_draws` held the defender's facing for one frame, which an NPC off its beat does not read (its body turns to the aim it holds): it is held for four. `evade_frag_seen` was re-recorded as well (17 recordings in all). The slowest ask of an escape search under the beat (`EscapePool.ask`, the gap since the search was last asked about) was 2 frames in `escape_pool_spreads_plans` (1311 asks), `evade_wave_spots_match_own_plan` (80), `perf/grenade_match_stress` (233): 30 is `STALE_FRAMES`.

**Tidy after review (#194).** (1) `order_stack_go_latched` reads any `going_in` again: the beat's first cut had narrowed it to the back door; every `going_in` of the run printed on the code before the beat (cdf8b659) and on the tip is the two members' at the back door alone (23.63 s, ready 23.62 s; on the beat 24.18 s, ready 24.17 s; six runs on the tip identical), so the beat posts no going in at another door and the filter hid nothing. (2) Every wake is pinned: `ai/pace/npc_beats_on_its_frames` runs a hit on it, a hit near it on another victim, a grenade, an explosion, an order through the squad and a flash-blinding, each posted on a frame off the NPC's turn (the old case posted after a coast, a frame that was a beat anyway), and a far hit as the negative; `ai/gait_every_frame_suite` takes `needs_every_frame` clause by clause (throw, committed, entry at 199 and 201 px, a guard's detour, no doorway yet, past its approach, door worked, door detour, evade, primed, blind). Each clause dropped in turn (the twelve of `behavior_gait.gd`, the four `urgent` sites, `_was_blind`) fails a case; `blind_pending` no longer reads `is_blind()` (the gait does), which no case could tell apart. (3) `ai/pace/beat_phases_split_a_match`: a 10v10 match's twenty NPCs fall within two of an even split over the two phases.
